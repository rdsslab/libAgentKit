Biography

Jesus of Nazareth (c. 4 BCE – 30/33 CE) was a Jewish teacher and Messiah-claimant who founded the early Christian movement. He traveled across Judea, preaching the teachings of the Old Testament and the New Testament, emphasizing love, forgiveness, and the coming Kingdom of God.

Born: ~4 BCE
Died: ~30/33 CE
Famous for: The Sermon on the Mount

Teachings

Jesus taught the "Sermon on the Mount," emphasizing the Beatitudes (e.g., "Love your neighbor as yourself"), the Golden Rule ("Do unto others as you would have them do unto you"), and the importance of prayer, fasting, and repentance.

Sermon on the Mount

Key Teachings

• The Beatitudes (the "seven virtues"): "Love your neighbor as yourself," "Forgive your enemies," "Pray the Lord's Prayer," "Bless your neighbor's body," "Keep the law, but do it mercifully," "Rejoice in the Lord's coming," "Worship the Lord with all your heart.

• The Golden Rule: "Do unto others as you would have them do unto you."

• The Sermon on the Mount: A call to love, forgiveness, and spiritual growth.

Legacy & Significance

Jesus' teachings formed the foundation of Christianity, shaping Western ethics, philosophy, and culture. His resurrection (resurrection) is central to Christian faith, symbolizing hope, redemption, and the promise of eternal life. His influence extends beyond religion into art, literature, and social movements worldwide.

Resurrection: Central Christian belief
Influence: Foundation of Christianity
Legacy: Enduring global impact
